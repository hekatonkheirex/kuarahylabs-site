A medication log is a reference tool. It helps you keep the information supplied by a prescribing clinic together so it can be reviewed accurately at an appointment. It is not a substitute for the prescription label or professional guidance.

## What to record

Use the medication name as shown on the label, the prescribing veterinarian or clinic, the start or change date, and the label instructions. Keep refill information and the original prescription documents where they can be found. If an instruction changes, record the date and source of the new instruction.

You can also keep a factual administration history: the date and time you recorded, plus a note identifying a missed or uncertain entry. Do not use a log to decide what to do about a missed entry, a changed schedule, or a dose. Contact the prescribing veterinarian or pharmacist with those questions.

## Observations belong in context

Write observations in plain language and include the date. A note such as “less interested in food this morning” is clearer than a conclusion about the cause. Bring the log to the veterinarian and ask what information is relevant. Do not interpret an observation as an adverse reaction or treatment instruction.

## Keep documents with the log

Prescription labels, written clinic instructions, and refill documents are the source material. A digital log is useful for an overview, while the original paperwork preserves the exact wording. Store documents securely and keep names and dates consistent across your record.

## Common documentation mistakes

- Recording a memory instead of the label information.
- Leaving out the prescribing clinic or change date.
- Mixing different medications into one unclear note.
- Guessing what a missed entry means.
- Updating the record without preserving the original instruction.
