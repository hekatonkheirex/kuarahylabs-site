Cat vaccination records are most useful when they are complete, legible, and easy to bring to a veterinary conversation. They document what a clinic recorded; they are not a substitute for an individualized vaccination assessment.

## What to keep

Keep certificates, clinic summaries, and any documentation that identifies the cat, the clinic, the date, and the vaccination information supplied. File the original paperwork and add a short digital summary with the document date and source. This makes it easier to locate the original if a facility asks for it.

## Why records matter

Records can be useful for routine veterinary visits, boarding, travel, or a change of caregiver. They also help a veterinarian understand what documentation exists and where there are gaps. A clear record should show uncertainty plainly rather than making an assumption about a missing entry.

## Core and non-core terminology

You may hear vaccination categories described as core or non-core. These are high-level terms, not a checklist for every cat. Indoor and outdoor lifestyle, kitten or adult history, health, local conditions, travel, and regulations may affect what a licensed veterinarian discusses with you. Indoor living does not remove the need for an individual veterinary assessment.

## Keep requests practical

When a boarding provider or travel authority asks for paperwork, ask exactly which document and dates it requires. Requirements may vary by location and organization. Bring the original record when requested, and check details with the relevant provider rather than assuming an old certificate applies.

## Recovering a missing record

Start with the clinic that may have issued it. Ask whether it can provide a copy and whether there is identifying information it needs to locate the file. If the record cannot be found, let your current veterinarian know. Do not create a replacement record from memory.
